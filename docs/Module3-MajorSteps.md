## Step Summaries

### Step 0: Background
Popups explain that 9 reactions at equilibrium are perturbed in the full lab (this module simulates #1), define physical vs. chemical change, and state Le Chatelier's Principle.

### Step 0b: Safety First — Put On PPE
Press buttons at the PPE station to auto-equip goggles, lab coat, and gloves (hands turn blue). Popups cover corrosive acid/base (≥ 1 M) safety, metal ion waste handling, and that this is a *qualitative* experiment (detailed observations matter, exact amounts do not).

### Part 1, Reaction #1
The chromate–dichromate ion equilibrium.

### Step 1: Gather Equipment
Collect all items and place them in the work zone; locate the reagent bottles in the acid/base trays in the fume hood; label two test tubes. Gate: all equipment collected.

### Step 2: Measure 1 mL of 0.1 M potassium chromate
Pour yellow K₂CrO₄ via graduated cylinder into test tubes #1 and #2; flow auto-stops at 1 mL. Gate: both tubes filled.

### Step 3: Drop-wise addition of reagents
Pick up the 1 M NaOH bottle and its dropper, hold the dropper over test tube #1, and press the button to add drops; the system auto-stops at 4 drops. Gate: 4 drops added with stirring.

### Step 4: Stir the solution
Stir test tube #1 with the glass rod (circular hand motion, or hold a button for auto-stir) for at least 5 seconds.

### Step 5: Record your observations
Pick a physical vs. chemical change answer (retry until correct), then record the observation in the lab notebook by selecting pre-written statements.

### Step 6: Add 3 M sulfuric acid to test tube #2
Same procedure as steps 3–5 with 3 M H₂SO₄.

### Step 7: Add 1 M sodium hydroxide to test tube #2
Same procedure as steps 3–5 with 1 M NaOH.

### Waste Disposal and Wrap-up
Waste reminder: metal ion waste never goes down the drain; acid/base waste goes in designated containers; test tubes go in glass waste. Closing explanation: NaOH removes H⁺, shifting the equilibrium toward yellow chromate (CrO₄²⁻) rather than orange dichromate (Cr₂O₇²⁻); no precipitate forms.
